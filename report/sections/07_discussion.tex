\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What produced the accuracy.}
The largest single jump came from the training recipe rather than the
architecture: the baseline at 94.76\% became 97.36\% with long training,
TrivialAugment, Cutout, mixup/CutMix, SAM, and EMA. KD added another 0.4 points,
and the wider student added about 0.2. The 4.88M student ends up better than the
28.5M PyramidNet teacher and within 0.3 points of the best teacher pool. This is
consistent with the view that a long, well-regularized student can absorb the
teacher ensemble's function without inheriting its capacity. Architecture search
inside the 5M budget was secondary: the best shape is only 0.24 points above the
baseline shape at 300 epochs.

\paragraph{The gap we could not explain.}
Our best holdout number is 97.94\% (5-crop); the selected submissions scored
0.911 and 0.904 public, and the team's private score was 0.880. The private score
is roughly ten points below the holdout number, and the gap between the best
public score (0.911) and the private score (0.880) is 3.1 points. The
public/private split is 1,000 images each, so part of the 3.1-point difference is
sampling noise; the rest could be a harder private subset, a different capture
distribution, or both. We cannot separate these causes.

The 2,000 unlabeled images released with the starter data looked like natural
photographs and were clearly unlike CIFAR-10. They motivated a natural-image
shift hypothesis and a robustness program. They were not, however, the final
evaluation images. This is the most important methodological lesson of the
project: a proxy can be visually compelling and statistically well behaved and
still be the wrong proxy. We should have verified what the released sample was
before building a narrative around it. The analysis pipeline remains useful
(it is reproducible and we would reuse it), but its conclusions do not transfer
to the final test set.

\paragraph{Why AugMix was not shipped.}
AugMix is the one robustness intervention that survived every check we ran: it is
clean-neutral at 300 epochs, improves the composite by 2.2 points, improves
photographic jitter by 1.1--1.9 points, replicates across seeds, and keeps its
effect under three-teacher KD. It was still not in the final submission. The KD
confirmation finished after the team had selected its submissions; its logs stop
at epoch 178/300, and the full-data 1,200-epoch AugMix run was never trained.
Given that the final private gap is unexplained, we cannot claim that shipping
AugMix would have improved the score. The honest conclusion is that it was the
best-supported robustness candidate we had, and the submission schedule did not
allow us to test it at the shipping scale.

\paragraph{Rules and teacher size.}
The official overview constrains the submitted model to 5M trainable parameters
and prohibits pretrained weights and external data. It does not constrain the
size of auxiliary teachers. Our teachers are 28.5M and 36.5M parameters, trained
from scratch on the same 50,000 images; only the 4.88M student is submitted. We
state this explicitly because the budget is easy to misread as applying to every
model trained along the way.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Most comparisons use a single seed; the exceptions are the two AugMix seeds and
the ASAM confirm. The holdout is 5,000 images, so the seed-to-seed spread is
0.2--0.4 points and small differences should not be over-interpreted. The B5 KD
comparison is provisional at 200/300 epochs and was interrupted twice. The
corruption and photographic proxies are synthetic and label-free; they are stress
tests, not models of the final shift. The public and private leaderboards are
1,000 images each, so the leaderboard itself is noisy. We did not run fresh
1,200-epoch ASAM, SWA, or LS-KD experiments, and we did not test DKD or
structural teacher pruning. Finally, the full-data models have no unbiased
validation score, so the final selection relies on the holdout models and the
team's judgment.

\paragraph{What we would do differently.}
First, verify what any released test sample actually is before building a shift
story around it. Second, ablate augmentation at the shipping schedule, not at a cheap pilot: the
150-epoch pilot overstated AugMix's effect because the 300-epoch control improved
so much. Third, keep per-epoch logs for every run in
the repository; several curves in this paper are missing because the logs stayed
on a cluster or a cloud volume. Fourth, complete the KD robustness run before
submission selection, so that a clean-neutral augmentation has a chance to ship.
Fifth, treat the public leaderboard as a noisy estimate and select by paired
holdout comparisons, which is what the team did.
