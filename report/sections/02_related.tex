\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Efficient residual networks.}
Residual connections \citep{he2016deep} made very deep convolutional networks
trainable. Wide Residual Networks \citep{zagoruyko2016wide} showed that width is
a more efficient axis than depth at a fixed parameter count, and PyramidNet
\citep{han2017deep} grows channels gradually while using parameter-free shortcuts;
ShakeDrop regularizes the resulting deep networks.
Our students are a pre-activation bottleneck ResNet family with 4.88--4.92M
parameters; the teacher ensemble uses WRN-28-10 and PyramidNet-110-270.

\paragraph{Optimization and regularization.}
Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) \citep{foret2021sharpness} and its adaptive
variant ASAM \citep{kwon2021asam} seek flat minima by perturbing weights before
each update. Weight averaging, both EMA and SWA, is a cheap way to stabilize
stochastic training. Cosine schedules with warmup and label smoothing are
standard. We combine SAM, EMA, cosine decay, and label smoothing, and we test
ASAM/SWA as late fine-tunes.

\paragraph{Data augmentation.}
TrivialAugment \citep{muller2021trivialaugment} samples a single operation and
magnitude per image and is competitive without tuning. AutoAugment
\citep{cubuk2019autoaugment} and RandAugment \citep{cubuk2020randaugment} search
or parameterize augmentation policies; Cutout \citep{devries2017cutout}, mixup
\citep{zhang2018mixup}, and CutMix \citep{yun2019cutmix} regularize by occluding
or mixing images. AugMix \citep{hendrycks2021augmix} mixes multiple
augmentation chains and adds a Jensen-Shannon divergence consistency loss; in
our implementation we use the image-only, one-view variant without JSD. We
compare these policies at matched schedules and on clean and shifted proxies.

\paragraph{Knowledge distillation.}
KD \citep{hinton2015distilling} trains a small student on the softened outputs of
one or more teachers. Long schedules and consistent teacher/student views are
important for state-of-the-art distillation. Logit-standardized KD (LS-KD) \citep{sun2024logit} removes per-example scale and
shift before matching distributions and reports better calibration. We use
ordinary KL distillation with two or three teachers and evaluate LS-KD as a
matched alternative.

\paragraph{Distribution shift and test-time adaptation.}
CIFAR-10.1 \citep{recht2018cifar} showed that natural
distribution shifts can produce large accuracy drops even for models that
generalize well in-distribution. The common-corruption benchmark
\citep{hendrycks2019benchmarking} provides synthetic stress tests; covariate-shift
adaptation attempts to close the gap without labels. Sinkhorn balancing is a
related class-prior correction. We use corrupted
and photometric proxies as stress tests, and we evaluate BN adaptation and
Sinkhorn balancing as negative results. Crucially, the unlabeled sample available
to us was not the final evaluation set, so we do not claim that any of these
proxies models the actual shift.
